# Phishing and Credential Theft Threat Advisory

## Overview
Phishing attacks are malicious attempts to acquire sensitive information, such as usernames, passwords, credit card details, and bank account credentials, by masquerading as a trustworthy entity in electronic communication. In these campaigns, scammers typically clone the user interfaces of major banks, e-commerce giants, or streaming platforms and use deceptive domains. Advanced phishing now uses homograph attacks, typosquatting (e.g. g00gle.com), and excessive subdomains to hide the true threat origin.

## Warning Signs
* The domain name contains unusual dashes, numbers, or misspelled brand names.
* The website forces immediate login to resolve an "urgent" account issue.
* The URL ends with low-reputation or free Top-Level Domains (TLDs) like .xyz, .top, .cfd, or .win.
* The page explicitly asks for OTPs, PINs, or secondary authentication tokens.

## Prevention Guidance
* Always manually type the official website address into your browser rather than clicking email or SMS links.
* Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) via authenticator apps (not SMS) on all crucial accounts.
* Check the website's SSL certificate, but remember that scammers can easily obtain free SSL certificates.
* Never download or execute attachments from unexpected sources that require immediate attention.
